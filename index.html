The benchmark

Given a complete reference video, an Agent builds an editable, animated Blender scene that reproduces it. CineRecon evaluates this task under three reconstruction needs:

Plan layout and motion

Proxy

Use simple geometric stand-ins to reproduce where subjects are, how they move and interact, and how the camera films them.

Keep subjects recognizable

Simplified

Preserve recognizable shapes and key appearance attributes, while simplifying geometry and surface detail.

Reproduce visual detail

Detailed

Recreate the reference’s distinctive shapes, materials, and fine visual details.

All three settings require the same subject motion, actions, and camera behavior. They differ in how much visual detail the scene must reproduce.

How are reconstructions scored?

We compare the rendered video with the reference over short evaluation clips. Each checklist item specifies something that should be visible or happen in that clip. Scores cover three equally weighted dimensions:

Camera consistency

Does the camera occupy the right position, point in the right direction, and reproduce the reference’s movement, zoom, and shot cuts?

Static consistency

Are the required subjects present, with the appearance expected for the chosen setting and the correct spatial relationships?

Dynamic consistency

Do subjects follow the right motion and perform the required actions and interactions at the right times, such as reaching down and placing a hand on the ground?

What remains difficult?

Inspect individual checklist items

Models share weaknesses in spatial relations, specific action events, and camera changes. Simplifying appearance requirements improves camera and dynamic consistency for most models, but errors in action timing, contact relations, and coordinated subject motion persist under Proxy.
